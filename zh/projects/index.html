📅 2026/最新研究#

Health is the new safety: Destination public health vulnerability, resilience, and international tourism#

Authors: Yibin Berry Zhou, Zhiguang Huang, Chen Jason Zhang, Menglong Xia#

Journal: Journal of Destination Marketing & Management [SSCI Q1, IF = 7.4]

Figure: Overview of Research Framework.

Tourism is highly vulnerable to health crises, yet little is known about how structural public health risks shape long-term demand. This study develops a composite Destination Public Health Vulnerability (DPHV) index that integrates exposure, sensitivity, and adaptive capacity, and links it to international tourism demand. Drawing on a global panel of 196 countries from 2000 to 2019, two-way fixed effects models, distributed lag models, and spatial Durbin models are employed, complemented by robustness checks leveraging the 2003 SARS outbreak. Results show that higher DPHV significantly reduces per capita tourist arrivals, with effects persisting for up to three years, underscoring the enduring impact of health-related risk perceptions. Resilience features, including cultural heritage and sanitation infrastructure, partially buffer these negative effects, whereas political stability provides a comparatively weaker safeguard. Spatial Durbin models reveal that health shocks primarily depress demand domestically rather than diffusing broadly across neighboring destinations. By conceptualizing public health vulnerability as a multidimensional construct and linking it to global tourism demand, this study advances destination vulnerability theory and highlights the role of resilience assets in mitigating health risks. These insights offer practical guidance for policymakers and destination managers seeking to sustain competitiveness in an increasingly crisis-prone global tourism system.

APA Citation:

Zhou, Y. B., Huang, Z., Zhang, C. J., & Xia, M. (2026). Health is the new safety: Destination public health vulnerability, resilience, and international tourism. Journal of Destination Marketing & Management, 41, 101121.

Keywords: Public health; Destination vulnerability; Destination resilience; Tourism demand; Econometrics; Crisis management.

📅 2025研究#

Unveiling Customer Engagement in Destination Marketing: A Multimodal Approach to Analyzing Official Promotional Videos#

Authors: Yibin Berry Zhou, Chen Jason Zhang, Menglong Xia#

Journal: International Journal of Tourism Research [SSCI Q1, IF = 5.7]

Figure: Overview of Research.

This study examines how features of official destination promotional videos (DPVs) shape customer engagement (CE) on social media. Using 5,037 Instagram videos from 54 U.S. national parks, we apply machine learning and computer vision to extract video content, visual features, multimodal cues (text, audio, thumbnails), and post metadata. LightGBM with SHAP analyses shows that thumbnail–video contrasts, color coherence, and emotional cues significantly influence CE. Simpler, familiar content drives passive engagement (likes), whereas emotionally resonant, high-aesthetic content fosters active engagement (comments). Beyond method, this study contributes a dual-pathway theoretical framework that integrates cognitive load, elaboration likelihood, and media richness theories to explain how multimodal features jointly shape CE. Practical implications highlight balancing content simplicity and depth, ensuring visual and emotional consistency, and optimizing posting strategies. Overall, this research advances destination marketing by providing a scalable and theory-informed framework for analyzing social media videos.

APA Citation:

Zhou, Y. B., Zhang, C. J., & Xia, M. (2025). Unveiling customer engagement in destination marketing: A multimodal approach to analyzing official promotional videos. International Journal of Tourism Research, 27(6), e70152.

Keywords: Customer engagement; Video analysis; Destination promotional video; Multimodal; Tourism destination marketing; Machine learning.
